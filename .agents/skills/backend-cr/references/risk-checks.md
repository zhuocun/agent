# Risk Checks

Apply only clauses whose risks are reachable in the authorized impact closure. For each applicable check, record its trigger, contract or sourced limit, evidence, result, and missing proof. The presence of one risk does not activate every clause in its section. Prefer existing reliable capabilities. These checks do not authorize operational actions or policy changes.

## Value and Time Boundaries

- **Numbers.** Trace input, computation, conversion, and persistence. Verify type, units, scale, precision, rounding, bounds, exponent forms, overflow, division by zero, and error precedence against the authoritative contract. Require final persisted values to satisfy the constraint and conversions not to bypass it. Do not impose historical constants, exact decimal arithmetic on every numeric use, or unsupported restrictions on legitimate intermediate values.
- **Text.** Check whitespace, case, locale, boundary characters, runtime character definitions, and storage collation across affected entries. Verify the contracted equivalence relation and whether original text must survive normalization. Require fixed mappings and cross-version checks only when cross-environment consistency is promised. Do not normalize every field alike.
- **Time and measurement.** Distinguish instants, local calendar dates, elapsed durations, interval endpoints, length units, and character encodings. Verify explicit time basis, zone, precision, controllable current time, cross-day and seasonal-clock boundaries, and strict input fidelity. Use reliable elapsed time for local budgets, never compare unrelated process-local clocks across nodes, and do not replace calendar arithmetic with fixed durations.
- **Cross-node expiry.** When expiry, leases, scheduling, or cleanup depends on clocks across nodes, identify time authority, allowed skew, and forward/backward jump behavior. Verify acceptance and expiry within the promised skew. Do not infer global order from local timestamp ordering, claim arbitrary-skew safety from normal synchronization, or silently extend a security lifetime outside the guarantee. Check the contractual rejection, isolation, or effect restriction beyond that guarantee.
- **Collections.** Check capacity, grouping, null-element counting, deduplication identity, atomic comparison of compound identities, and original positions in business units. Exercise exact boundaries and excess cases that expose filtering, cross-group totals, or incorrect identity. Do not substitute display names, transfer one collection limit to another, or split batches in a way that changes whole-set semantics.

## Concurrency and Deduplication

- **Decision protection.** For mutable read-dependent invariants, enumerate read and write sets, cross-object constraints, ranges, and absence checks. Identify what protects each fact until its effect becomes effective. Exercise an interleaving where both prechecks succeed to expose write skew, lost updates, or stale decisions. An object revision or consistent snapshot alone does not prove a cross-object invariant. Do not assume a single-record atomic guarantee covers multiple records, partitions, or replicas.
- **Shared state and progress.** For concurrent mutable memory, verify runtime synchronization and publication visibility; storage transactions do not protect unrelated memory. When scoped work acquires or waits for locks, execution slots, connections, leases, or child work, trace acquisition and waiting dependencies. Check cycles, waiting on work that needs the same occupied resource, and starvation under supported scheduling. Verify contracted completion or recovery under controlled healthy workloads separately from invariant preservation. Repeated bounded timeouts do not prove progress. Prefer existing ordering, ownership, and scheduling rules; do not add coordination to isolated immutable computation.
- **Duplicate suppression.** When suppression is required or implemented, check operation/security-domain identity, effect-relevant input binding, concurrent ownership, in-progress states, result persistence, and retention against the promised guarantee. Reject or contractually distinguish different input under one identity. Do not return an in-progress record as completed success. For a no-repeat/replay promise, verify effect/result interruption recovery and retention covering the promised window, including later replay handling. Do not create a deduplication facility without that requirement.

## Asynchronous Work and Recovery

- **Asynchronous authorization.** Distinguish interactive requests, delayed delegated commands, and notifications of completed facts. Verify trusted producer, service identity, target security domain, delegation scope, and replay boundary. Payload identity or tenant alone is not authority. For delayed or replayed commands, check the contracted authorization time, revocation, and expiry. For completed facts, verify origin and processing permission rather than mechanically demanding that the original actor still has initiation permission. A trusted channel is not unrestricted business authority.
- **Acknowledgment and handoff.** Enumerate acknowledgment, consumer progress, business effects, retry transfer, and final-failure handling in order. Check each acknowledgment against its contracted completion or acceptance meaning. Where durable handling is promised, require the effect or reliable recoverable handoff before acknowledgment; function return, an in-memory queue, or asynchronous submission alone is insufficient. Exercise partial batches and interruption at each handoff. Do not advance progress over work the contract requires retaining or rebuild an already verified delivery facility.
- **Ordering and ownership.** Derive duplicate, loss, and replay guarantees from the business contract, not transport terminology. For effects that must not repeat, verify effective protection across interruption and redelivery. For permitted repeats or loss windows, verify scope, consequences, and recovery without strengthening the promise. Prevent stale snapshot overwrite without discarding required increments. For noncommutative commands/events, identify the required partial order, routing identity, actual ordering scope, and sequence evidence. Exercise gaps, missing predecessors, reordering, and late arrival against contracted waiting, rejection, reconciliation, or recovery. Distinguish event time from processing time when behavior depends on time; partitions and timestamps do not prove global causal order. Do not order safely commuting effects. Where work ownership expires, require final-effect identity/generation guards so stale workers cannot write or release successor ownership. Verify promised terminal-failure retention and actionable handling using existing facilities.
- **Durable recovery.** For reachable external-effect windows or accepted durable recovery obligations, mark interruption points at external calls, local commit, outcome recording, compensation, and notification. Where recovery is required, verify identities, intent, and facts survive process loss or can be reconstructed from durable authority. Distinguish not executed, executed, and unknown outcomes, including failed, repeated, and unknown compensation. For manual handling, identify detection conditions, evidence, accountable entry point, and executable steps; a named owner alone is insufficient. Keep explicitly accepted loss windows visible rather than silently adding a stronger recovery promise.
- **Resource ownership.** Trace bounded queues and waits, observation of asynchronous failures, owned execution resources, and borrowed/shared resources through errors, early returns, nested scopes, and shutdown. Require owned resources to close and borrowed ones to remain under their owner. Restore outer context after nested exit. A fixed reference or bounded queue alone does not prove lifetime correctness or prevent request-context leakage.

## Queries, Budgets, and Capacity

- **Executed queries.** At the authorized maximum scale, inspect actual executed statements, bindings and their types, controlled parameter sources, read/write predicates, returned volume, query counts, and execution plans. Apply authorization before contractual counts and pagination. Verify stable ordering, cursor behavior, and the honest consistency boundary under concurrent changes. Permit application filtering only with authorized bounds, suitable access paths, and no per-item fetch amplification. Builder code, an index declaration, or a tiny sample is not execution evidence.
- **Remaining budget.** Deduct queueing, processing, and backoff from the original request/task budget using reliable elapsed time. Before new requested work or retry, recompute the remainder, propagate an interpretable deadline, and cap waiting at the lesser of dependency limit and remainder. With nonpositive remainder, start no new requested business work; take the contracted bounded failure or degradation exit. Do not suppress required cleanup, cancellation, unknown-outcome reconciliation, or an accepted durable recovery obligation. Give that work a separate bounded budget and explicit owner, or a reliable handoff to existing recovery. Do not extend the caller deadline or authorize new requested effects through this exception. Test expiry before cleanup, during unknown outcomes, and after durable acceptance, including handoff failure. Retries receive no fresh full request budget; small remainders must not become API values meaning unlimited waiting.
- **Total cost.** Where resource use can change, record applicable request rate, concurrency, payload bytes and depth, data distribution, and hot spots with their sources. Trace parsing, decompression, computation, serialization, lock holding, result retention, and competing background work. Require limits before expensive operations and assess total amplification, not merely per-item or per-page bounds. Use credible estimates, reusable evidence, or authorized representative measurements to check capacity and overload. Do not infer total performance from a query plan or require full load testing for unrelated changes.
- **Overload and retry multiplication.** Compute combined attempts across the complete call chain. Verify concurrency, queueing, waits, and backoff against actual resource constraints and contractual rejection, isolation, or degradation. Account for synchronized retries where relevant. Work still running after timeout remains resource consumption. Propagate supported cancellation and do not claim unsupported cancellation stopped effects. No retry with prompt failure may be correct. Do not automatically introduce retries, circuit breaking, or a new limiting service.

## Caches and Diagnostics

- **Existing caches.** Verify keys include every result-changing dimension. Distinguish positive, negative, dependency-failure, expired, and approved stale results. Check invalidation, rollback visibility, and racing fills so an old fill cannot overwrite newer facts. Expiry alone does not establish strong freshness. Cross-request caching must not replace required current authorization. Do not create a cache or a cross-request invalidation protocol for simple request-local immutable reuse.
- **Offline diagnostics.** Preserve invalid raw facts without expanding legal domain states. In an authorized investigation, record independently decidable row anomalies and continue other decidable rows while preserving online corruption rejection. Stop on connection failure or unknown decisive facts. Do not generalize continue-after-recording to online writes or repair the investigation target without authorization.

## Technical Security and Data Protection

- **Dangerous operations.** Trace untrusted data to query, network, file, process, and deserialization operations within scope. Verify separation of values from executable structure and constraints on dynamic targets under actual execution semantics. Login, business validation, surface string checks, or parameter binding alone does not establish target safety. Do not apply indiscriminate sanitization to every ordinary field or expand into a whole-system security audit.
- **Sensitive data.** Trace actual sensitive-data recipients and flows through transport, persistence, caches, samples, backups, access, and deletion. Verify required peer authentication and transport protection cannot silently disappear under debug settings. Require replaceable secrets rather than hardcoded unrotatable long-term credentials. Use established capabilities and the approved classification. Do not invent cryptographic protocols, impose identical protection on all data, or build new key infrastructure without need.

## Migration, Release, and Configuration

- **Live migration.** For actual retained data, check locks, load, business waits, concurrent new writes, progress, interruption, bounded batches, safe reentry, and stop conditions as relevant. Prevent backfill from replacing newer facts. Reconcile data and business invariants, not only record counts. Simplify empty-store initialization or authorized small offline migrations. Do not override an explicit no-migration decision or require all migrations to run online.
- **Destructive conversion and recovery.** Identify irreversible points and the promised recovery point, duration, and allowed loss. Verify usable original representations, recoverable copies, or lossless reverse transformations against that promise and concurrent later writes. A backup declaration is not restore proof. For authorized forward-only recovery, record unrecoverable loss, approval boundary, stop conditions, and executable steps. Recovery must not silently overwrite later legitimate writes or exceed allowed loss. Perform any recovery exercise only on authorized isolated resources.
- **Release and rollback.** Distinguish process survival, traffic readiness, and business correctness using actual artifacts, configuration, and business signals. Verify failure stop conditions, reachable code/configuration/data/message combinations, and preservation of promised in-flight work during switching and shutdown. Require evidence that an old version can read new data and handle outstanding work before calling rollback viable. Otherwise record limits and executable forward recovery, never erase new data to claim rollback. Do not require a new staged-release platform for a simple release.
- **Configuration transitions.** Verify legal, default, missing, and invalid values and both branches of actual switches. For dynamic changes, identify request-local configuration version, safe mixed versions, and treatment of queued/in-flight work. Switching off must stop prohibited effects, not just hide entry points. Switching back does not undo durable effects. Use startup validation for static settings where sufficient. Do not add dynamic configuration, switches, or governance templates without a requirement.

## Logs and Operational Detection

- **Safe attributable logs.** At the final boundary, verify failure class, necessary safely handled identifiers, and security reasons can be correlated. Prevent secret or complete sensitive-payload leakage, record forgery through control characters, and unbounded fields or whole records. Centralized logging can suffice. Local redaction or a logging-level guard does not authorize full sensitive messages, and every layer need not repeat the event.
- **Detection and handoff.** Trigger when the affected path has a sourced detection duty, or silent failure could conceal an accepted durable write, external effect, or message/task completion. State the obligation and consequence that make the check applicable; unknown applicability remains a coverage gap. Check relevant success, business rejection, technical failure, latency, errors, saturation, and backlog. Identify observation target, aggregation/sampling, window, detection condition, and response owner/entry. Include no-progress and missing-telemetry cases where they can hide that failure. Use a known failure scenario to verify detection and diagnostic context against the sourced time requirement; if no time limit exists, record the missing owner decision instead of inventing one. Distinguish zero traffic, zero errors, and missing signals. Preserve safe correlation across difficult asynchronous/remote boundaries and bound metric dimensions. Metric existence or protocol success does not prove business health. Do not instrument every pure function, require a new platform, or make optional telemetry a universal startup gate.

## Dependency Upgrades

- **Changed dependency behavior.** Identify direct and transitive changes and inspect relevant release notes. Verify affected serialization, time, retry, transaction, exception, and connection behavior plus reproducible builds. Relate vulnerabilities to reachable runtime conditions and leave unknown exposure unknown. Compilation or a small version increment is not behavioral proof. Reuse trusted build and scanning capabilities rather than upgrading unrelated dependencies or creating a supply-chain platform.
