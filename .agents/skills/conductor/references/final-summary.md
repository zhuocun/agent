# Final summary

Read this file in full before writing the final summary, and again after a compaction before writing it. **Communication** in `SKILL.md` says when the summary is written.

Write the final summary for a reader who saw none of the working thread: a re-grounding, not a continuation of it.

Drop the working thread's shorthand. Write complete sentences. Spell out terms. Don't use arrow chains, hyphen-stacked compounds, or labels you made up mid-run. When you mention files, commits, flags, or other identifiers, give each one its own plain-language clause.

Take the same order every time. The task, in one or two bullets naming what the reader asked for in their words rather than the run's. The outcome, in one sentence on what happened or what was found. The definition of done as a checklist — the one you fixed before dispatch, plus the items the user approved since, never one written afterwards to fit the result — with every ticked item naming the evidence that proves it, and every unticked item saying what is missing and why. The result against any stretch target. Any risk the checklist does not already carry. What remains as open work. Last, and separately, each thing you need from the reader — anything that blocks the work or needs their decision — explained as if new; if you need nothing, ask nothing.

- [ ] The checklist in the summary is the definition of done fixed before dispatch, plus the items the user approved since, never one written to fit the result; every ticked item names its evidence, every unticked item says what is missing, and no stretch target was counted as part of it.
