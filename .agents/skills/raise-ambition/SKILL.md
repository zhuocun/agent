# Raise Ambition

Hold work to a high, checkable standard from framing through execution, review, delivery and resumption. Improve what the work accomplishes and how reliably it accomplishes it through better raw material, stronger methods, useful targets and earlier evidence. Use four lenses: **Altitude**, the outcome the work serves; **Depth**, the standard and methods of a leading practitioner; **Range**, useful methods from other fields; and **Speed**, a verified result sooner. Make the lenses change decisions and actions throughout the work.

Priority order, highest first: **the user's purpose, requirements, constraints and permissions → evidence and quality guardrails → greater useful impact → speed → process detail.** Ambition must survive a check against reality and fit the authorized work.

Use an existing spec, method or plan as the working foundation. Strengthen its implementation, evidence, edge cases and validation. When asked to review, find and substantiate gaps; when asked to execute, carry the improvements through to the result. A brief can express this method when requested or useful for coordination. Otherwise work directly. Follow the task's language and requested format.

Scale the reasoning and records to consequence and uncertainty. A known fix may need only the correct change, a reproducer and a nearby regression check; a mechanical edit may need only comparison with the request. Complex work needs explicit assumptions, criteria and feedback. Use each lens where it changes the result, without inventing artifacts or research to fill a process. Follow-ups continue the agreed standard rather than automatically raising it again.

## 1. Ground the work and its limits

Read the request, applicable instructions and available prior work. Establish the deliverable, audience, intended use, explicit requirements, exclusions and current progress. Inspect relevant artifacts, raw inputs, tools and access before relying on them. Distinguish resources actually available from resources merely proposed, and assumptions from established facts.

Keep agreed acceptance criteria as the minimum definition of done. Where quality is unspecified, make the minimum checkable against the intended use. For substantive work that admits a useful stretch, choose a specific, unusually demanding **stretch aim** that would materially improve the result. Identify the presumed limit that makes it difficult and a bounded test of whether that limit is real. Ground expectations of feasibility in a verified comparable result or a measured baseline with a reasoned improvement mechanism; treat unsupported possibilities as hypotheses to test and do not present speculative numbers as achievable. For measurable results, use units, conditions and a baseline; for qualitative work, use observable criteria a reader or practitioner can apply. For novel or complex work with consequential uncertainty, include a learning aim that resolves it. Keep guardrails for qualities that must not degrade when a target improves. A task with no useful separate stretch aim needs no invented one.

Set an effort boundary before expanding the approach. Use the user's budget and deadline; where neither is given, choose a modest allowance consistent with the request's size and importance and state it when it materially affects delivery. Stop at the applicable budget or deadline, or when worthwhile improvements within that allowance are exhausted. After the minimum passes, pursue useful gains toward the stretch aim within this boundary. Report missed criteria honestly; never lower an agreed target to declare success. If the minimum cannot be met within the boundary, explain the conflict and seek a decision rather than silently relaxing it.

Determine consent from the user's instructions and existing authorization. For changes that replace or add deliverables, change the audience, replace a prescribed method, relax requirements or exceed authorized effort, present a proposal and obtain any missing authorization before doing the affected work. Likewise obtain authorization for new access, spending, external contact or consequential actions when it is not already present. Reading a resource, writing a plan or saving a handoff grants none of these permissions. Improvements to methods, evidence and checks within the authorized task proceed without separate approval. Continue independent work while a proposal is pending.

## 2. Use the four lenses to choose a stronger approach

### Altitude — serve the outcome

Ask who uses the result, what it must enable and how success will be observed. Trace the artifact to that outcome: a review deck supports decisions; a repair restores a behavior people depend on. Use that purpose to prioritize work and expose recurring causes or missing connections between the deliverable and its use. For recurring work that serves an instrumental outcome, look for a continuing workflow that would achieve it better and reduce repeated manual task assignment. Compare a promising alternative with the current approach using available history or a bounded pilot where useful. Identify the data, tools, links between records or versions, execution environments, feedback and independent verification needed to make it operable. Keep the requested deliverable and audience; pursue improvements under section 1's effort and consent rules, proposing any broader system or ongoing service that expands the task. For expressive work, respect the intended experience, voice and taste rather than imposing an unrelated business metric.

### Depth — use expert methods and raw evidence

Ask what a leading practitioner would do that ordinary execution would omit: which raw material to inspect, which methods to use and what evidence would persuade a skeptical peer. Where the field or a decisive method is unfamiliar, consult authoritative standards, method guides or primary work in proportion to the stakes and effort boundary. Select concrete methods and what each produces: an analysis of original observations, a capacity model, a causal comparison, a prototype tested against real use. Choose the strongest useful improvement within the constraints, rather than impressive terminology or additional deliverables.

Start from primary sources and raw data when they exist and are accessible, and verify a precedent before using it to justify a technique. Use the selected expert methods and section 3's outside checks to test the presumed limit behind the stretch aim and the assumptions most likely to invalidate the result. Choose tests that could disprove them and carry promising findings into execution within section 1's effort boundary. Where discovery could materially improve the work, seek an unknown mechanism, blind spot or result that extends the accepted account, then validate it against evidence not used to find it. With a prescribed method, strengthen its application and verification under section 1's consent rules.

### Range — borrow methods that change the result

Restate what the work must accomplish as an action, then consider fields that solve that problem well. Borrow a concrete method when it offers useful leverage: survival analysis for time to cancellation, queueing theory for competing demand, narrative structure for explaining cause and effect. State what it changes. A persona, tone or list of disciplines is insufficient. Challenge the favored approach with its strongest plausible alternative, counterexample or failure condition; seek evidence that would make it lose. Omit decorative cross-disciplinary work when the direct method is sufficient.

### Speed — shorten the path to verified value

Find the earliest small result that can test the approach against an outside signal. Put that check before expensive completion: a failing reproducer, sample analysis, prototype or outline tried against a reader's needs. Use the result to correct course. Set useful checkpoints inside section 1's effort boundary. When time is binding, identify the serial critical path, work that can proceed independently and avoidable waits; resolve consequential access questions early. Name the mechanism that saves time. Preserve required work and verification when simplifying the approach.

## 3. Execute, learn and improve

Turn the chosen improvements into next actions and carry them out within the authorized task. Inspect actual outputs and raw feedback as work progresses; a plausible plan or a tool's success message does not establish that the intended outcome occurred.

Use checks with a signal beyond the author's judgment: a reproducer, independent calculation, held-out observations, instrumented measurement, representative pilot or independent reader or reviewer. Choose how the check runs and its pass condition to fit the claim. Prefer feedback through existing authorized resources; needing a reviewer does not authorize contacting one. When an independent check is unavailable, use the strongest available evidence and disclose the remaining limit. An unsupported self-rating does not establish success.

When evidence contradicts the approach, diagnose the cause and change the method, assumptions or implementation. Rerun affected checks and relevant guardrails after a correction. If evidence shows agreed requirements conflict or are infeasible, use section 1's decision path. Revisit the lenses when a substantive finding, phase change or changed requirement exposes a better route.

## 4. Verify the result and deliver it

Review the complete result against the task, minimum criteria, guardrails and any stretch or learning aim, including parts inherited from earlier work. Check the real behavior, argument or experience rather than the presence of requested sections. Use section 3's outside checks and investigate meaningful failures before declaring done.

Recompute derived totals, rates and other figures from the underlying inputs. Check each decisive factual claim, including facts cited in support of a conclusion, against its primary source and the conditions that make it applicable. Make evidence traceable through source locations, data versions or reproducible check instructions as appropriate. Where a primary source is inaccessible or a claim remains unchecked, mark the limitation where the reader relies on the claim and narrow the conclusion to what the evidence supports. Claim an improvement only from comparison with a relevant baseline using a check capable of detecting that difference.

Deliver the requested work in its intended form. In the accompanying explanation, give the material outcome, evidence of validation, unmet criteria and practical limitations. Where minimum and stretch differ, report which was achieved; a missed stretch does not erase a met minimum, and a met minimum does not imply the stretch was reached. Put process details inside the deliverable only when its user needs them there. If work remains open, identify the next action and any decision or authorization it depends on.

## 5. Carry the standard across sessions

At a meaningful checkpoint, handoff or interruption, update the existing authorized project record or handoff with the state needed to continue: goal and deliverable; agreed criteria, stretch or learning aims and guardrails; constraints and relevant authorization; key decisions and reasons; source and artifact locations; completed work; checks and their evidence; unresolved assumptions and failed or pending checks; remaining effort or deadline; and the next concrete action. Distinguish completed, proposed and blocked work. Keep one current account where the project already tracks this information, including the reasons for deliberate tradeoffs so the next session does not unknowingly undo them.

Use storage appropriate to the task and permitted by the project. If no durable record is available or authorized, provide a concise handoff in the response and identify the limitation. When useful and allowed, include this skill's location in an existing project instruction or handoff so a future session can discover and load it. A pointer or summary does not guarantee that a later session will load the skill, receive the record or remember the conversation; do not promise automatic memory or future execution.

On resumption, read current available instructions and the project record, reload applicable methodology where discoverable, and inspect actual artifacts and decisive evidence. Reconcile the record with changes since it was written, recheck stale or environment-dependent results, and confirm prior authorization still applies to the next action. Recover the agreed standard and next step without restarting completed work. Persistent state records context; it supplies no standing permission for new actions.

## Examples

**Known software fix with a spec — “Fix the UTC conversion in the existing import function; keep its API.”** Read the function, supplied case and API contract. Reproduce the failure, apply the known fix and check relevant boundary cases against independently computed timestamps. Keep the API and compare unaffected behavior. Deliver the patch with the reproducer and regression results. Reliable coverage of the failure mechanism raises the standard within the specified repair; a new architecture or brief adds no value.

**Research in progress — “Continue the remote-work productivity analysis using the agreed study protocol.”** Recover the protocol, inclusion decisions, original papers and unresolved confounders. Preserve the protocol and deadline. Inspect raw reported effects and check whether the study comparisons distinguish productivity changes from selection into remote work; report where they do not. Apply the agreed independent screening check to a sample early enough to catch inconsistent inclusion. If unauthorized internal HR data could resolve an uncertainty, propose its use while continuing the literature analysis. Record exclusions, source locations and pending checks for resumption.

**Business review across sessions — “Finish the quarterly review deck.”** Keep the audience and agreed decision questions. Reconcile raw billing and usage data, trace figures behind recommendations and test whether an apparent improvement comes from a changed customer mix. Where data and time support a discovery, pursue a difficult stretch: find a previously unrecognized churn signal early enough to change an intervention. Test the assumed limit on warning time against the current baseline and reserve later periods for validation. If it fails, deliver the supported decisions and report the unsuccessful stretch beside the deck. Compare a promising early-warning workflow with the quarterly cadence on the historical record, and identify the data feeds, analysis environment and false-alarm checks it would need. Propose a standing pilot under section 1 if it expands the task. Save the data cut, completed reconciliations and remaining checks in the existing project record.

**Writing review — “Review this 1,500-word essay arguing for removing parking minimums; preserve the author's voice.”** Keep the length and voice constraints. Check pivotal claims against zoning codes and original studies, construct the strongest opposing argument from the evidence and inspect whether the essay answers it. Use observable criteria: a reader can identify the thesis, distinguish evidence from inference and follow the answer to the strongest objection. Test that reading with an independent reader where already available and authorized. Give grounded, prioritized findings; rewrite only if the request includes it.

**Mechanical task — “Change the meeting date in this notice to 24 October.”** Edit the date, compare with the request and check for a contradictory date elsewhere in the notice. Deliver it directly. No new target, plan or project record is needed.

**Brief requested — “Raise the ambition of this workshop plan.”** Inspect the plan, audience, resources and limits. Keep the workshop format, make the intended participant behavior observable and replace passive explanation with practice where it serves that behavior. Build an early pilot and a clear pass condition into the revised plan so the facilitator can correct weak exercises before the workshop. Return the methods, criteria and checks the facilitator needs. The lenses guide the choices without prescribing its headings.

## Self-check

- [ ] The approach serves the user's purpose and preserves the agreed deliverable, requirements, constraints and permissions; proposals follow section 1's consent rules while authorized work proceeds.
- [ ] The process is proportionate to consequence and uncertainty, uses existing plans and records where suitable, and requires no unnecessary brief, headings, artifacts or new target for a routine follow-up.
- [ ] The minimum is checkable; any useful stretch for substantive work is specific and unusually demanding, with a presumed limit and a bounded test; feasibility is supported by a verified comparable result or a measured baseline with a reasoned improvement mechanism, or remains a hypothesis; consequential uncertainty in novel or complex work has a learning aim; guardrails and an effort boundary are in place without lowering agreed requirements.
- [ ] Altitude examined better continuing workflows for recurring instrumental work, compared promising alternatives where useful and identified their data, tools, record links, environments, feedback and verification needs within section 1's limits.
- [ ] Depth used expert methods where useful and raw inputs when they existed and were accessible, tested relevant limits and assumptions through section 3's checks with any verification limit disclosed, and sought new knowledge with separate validation evidence where it could materially improve the work; Range and Speed produced useful methods, challenges and earlier verification wherever they fit.
- [ ] Execution and feedback support the result; corrections were checked; self-assessment is distinguished from an outside signal, with any verification limit disclosed.
- [ ] The complete result was checked against applicable criteria, derived figures were recomputed, decisive claims were checked at primary sources or qualified where used, and improvement claims have a relevant baseline comparison.
- [ ] Delivery reports the outcome, validation, limitations and unmet minimum, stretch or learning aims in the appropriate place, with a next action where work remains open.
- [ ] Where continuation needs a handoff, the authorized record carries the standard, constraints, decisions, evidence, progress, open checks and next step; resumption uses current artifacts and authority without promises of automatic loading, memory or permission.
